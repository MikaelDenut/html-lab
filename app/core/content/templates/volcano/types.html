Three shapes of volcano

The lava decides the shape. Runny lava spreads out into wide, gentle volcanoes. Sticky lava, ash and lumps of lava pile up into steep ones. Point at a card, or press Tab, to fill it with lava.

Shield volcano

Wide and gentle

  • Runny lava flows a long way before it cools, so the sides are low and wide.
  • Mauna Loa in Hawaii is a shield volcano. It is the biggest active volcano on Earth.

Cinder cone

Small and steep

  • Lumps of lava fly out of one vent, cool in the air and pile up into a steep hill.
  • Parícutin in Mexico started in a farmer's cornfield in 1943.

Stratovolcano

Tall and steep

  • It is built from many layers of hardened lava and ash, one on top of another.
  • Mount Fuji in Japan is a stratovolcano. So is our Mount Cinderkettle!

Which volcano is it?

A small, steep hill with a bowl on top, made of lava lumps?

A cinder cone. The lumps landed all around the vent, like sand in an egg timer.

So wide and low, it looks like a shield lying on the ground?

A shield volcano. That look is where its name comes from.

Tall and steep, with stripes of lava and ash inside?

A stratovolcano. "Strata" means layers. Look for the stripes in our drawing on the first page.
