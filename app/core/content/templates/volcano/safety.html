How we keep watch

A volcano often gives signs before it erupts. We measure them every day, so people who live nearby can get ready in time.

What we measure

Ground shaking

Rising magma cracks the rock and makes small earthquakes. A seismometer feels shakes that are too small for people to notice.

Gas

Magma lets out gases, such as sulfur dioxide. If more gas comes out each day, magma may be moving up. Our gas sensors measure it.

Swelling

Magma pushing up can make the ground bulge, like a balloon under a carpet. GPS stations and tiltmeters (machines that feel the ground tip a tiny bit) notice even a tiny bulge.

Our alert colours

Real volcano observatories use colours like these to tell pilots how the volcano is doing.

  1. Green Quiet. A normal day.
  2. Yellow Restless. More shakes or gas than usual.
  3. Orange Very restless, or a small eruption has started.
  4. Red A big eruption with lots of ash is close or happening.

Volcano words

Magma
Melted rock under the ground.
Lava
Magma that has come out on top of the ground.
Ash
Tiny pieces of rock and glass thrown out by a volcano.
Eruption
When lava, ash or gas comes out of a volcano.
Active
Erupting now, or it erupted in about the last 10,000 years. For a volcano, that is not long ago!
Dormant
Sleeping. It has not erupted for a long time, but it could wake up.
Extinct
Finished. It is not expected to erupt ever again.
Seismometer
A machine that feels the ground shaking.
Volcanologist
A scientist who studies volcanoes.

Visiting a volcano park?

  • Stay on the marked paths. The ground near a vent can be thin and hot.
  • Read the signs and listen to the park rangers.
  • Keep away from steam and gas holes. Some volcano gases are bad to breathe.
