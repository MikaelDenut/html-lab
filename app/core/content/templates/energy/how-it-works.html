From wind to your plug

Electricity takes a long trip before it reaches your home. Here is every stop on the way.

The journey of electricity

Follow the arrows, from a puff of wind to the plug in your wall.

  1. Wind

    The wind pushes the three blades round and round.

  2. Generator

    The blades turn a shaft. Magnets spin past copper wire, and that makes electricity.

  3. Transformer

    It pushes the electricity much harder (that push is called voltage), so less of it is lost on the long trip.

  4. Power lines

    Tall towers carry the wires across the fields to the towns.

  5. Your plug

    A substation, a small fenced building full of machines, makes the push gentle again. Wires under the street bring it home.

And the sun?

Solar panels have no blades and no generator. The light does all the work.

  1. Sunlight

    Light from the sun reaches Earth in about 8 minutes.

  2. Solar panel

    Solar cells, mostly made of silicon, turn the light into electricity.

  3. Inverter

    The panel makes electricity that flows one way. The inverter changes it so it swings back and forth, like the electricity in your wall.

  4. Your home

    On a sunny day, lamps and fridges run on sunshine.

Did you know?

Before the blades start, the turbine turns its whole head to face the wind. A small wind vane on top tells it where the wind is coming from.
