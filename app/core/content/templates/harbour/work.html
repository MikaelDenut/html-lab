How a box moves

From the moment the ship arrives to the truck at the gate, there are six stops. Here is every one.

Six stops from ship to truck

Swipe sideways to see all six stops.

  1. Pilot and tugs

    A harbour pilot climbs aboard and guides the ship in. Strong little tugboats push it to its berth, its parking place at the quay.

  2. Ropes on bollards

    Thick ropes go from the ship to iron bollards on the quay, the stone edge of the harbour, so the ship cannot drift away.

  3. Crane and spreader

    The crane lowers its spreader, a steel frame that grabs a box, onto the box. Twistlocks, small turning locks, turn in the four corners and hold on tight.

  4. Straddle carrier

    A straddle carrier, a tall truck on long legs, drives right over the box, picks it up and takes it to the yard.

  5. Yard stack

    Boxes wait in long stacks. A computer remembers each box code and the exact spot of the box.

  6. Customs and gate

    Customs officers check what is inside and check the papers. At the gate a truck picks up the box and drives it away.

How do boxes hold on?

Every box has a strong steel corner with an oval hole. A twistlock fits into the hole and turns a quarter of the way round. Now the boxes are locked together, so they stay in place when the ship rolls.
