How we build a house

Eight big steps, one after the other. Most of them must wait for the step before.

The eight steps

  1. Plan and draw

    Weeks 1 to 8

    An architect draws every room. The town checks the plan and says yes.

  2. Clear and dig

    Week 9

    Diggers clear the ground and dig deep holes for the foundation.

  3. Pour the foundation

    Week 10

    Wet concrete fills the holes. It sets hard and holds the whole house up.

  4. Build the frame and walls

    Weeks 11 to 16

    The walls go up. Now you can walk through the rooms.

  5. Put on the roof

    Weeks 17 to 19

    The roof keeps the rain out, so the inside can stay dry.

  6. Pipes and wires

    Weeks 20 to 24

    Plumbers fit the water pipes. Electricians pull the wires for lights and plugs.

  7. Floors, walls and paint

    Weeks 25 to 32

    Floors go in, walls get smooth, and the painters add colour.

  8. Hand over the keys

    Week 33

    We check everything twice, tidy up and give the keys to the new family.

Site rules

  • Hard hat on, all the time.
  • Safety boots with steel toes.
  • A bright vest, so drivers can see you.
  • Never walk under a crane's load.
  • Visitors sign in at the site office.
  • Keep the site tidy. Tidy sites are safe sites.

Questions kids ask us

Why do cranes have big concrete blocks?

They are counterweights. The blocks sit on the short arm and balance the heavy load on the long arm, like a seesaw. Without them the crane would tip over.

What is a foundation?

It is the strong base under a house, mostly made of concrete. It spreads the weight of the house into the ground, so the house does not sink.

Why do builders wear bright vests?

Bright colours and shiny stripes are easy to see, even in rain or at dusk. Drivers and crane operators can spot every person on the site.

Why do builders test concrete after 28 days?

Concrete keeps getting harder for weeks, even months. After about a week it is strong enough to build on. Builders test a sample at 28 days to check it is as strong as the plan says.

How do bricks get so hard?

Bricks are made of clay. They are baked in a very hot oven called a kiln, at about 1000 degrees Celsius. The heat turns soft clay into something as hard as rock.

Can I visit a building site?

Not on your own: a building site is dangerous. Once a year we hold an open day, with small hard hats for kids and a ride in the digger seat.
