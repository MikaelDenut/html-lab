Dive to the bottom of the sea

The ocean is almost 11 km deep in its deepest place. Scroll down and sink with us. The deeper you go, the darker and colder it gets, and the harder the water presses on you.

Start the dive

0 mto 200 m

The sunlight zone

Sunlight reaches down here, so seaweed and tiny floating plants called phytoplankton can grow. They make about half of the oxygen on Earth. Most of the animals in the sea live in this zone.

Light
Bright sunshine
Pressure at 200 m
About 20 times more than at the top

200 mto 1,000 m

The twilight zone

Only a little blue light gets this far. It is too dark for plants to grow. Many animals here make their own light. This is called bioluminescence.

Light
A little blue
Pressure at 1,000 m
About 100 times more than at the top

1,000 mto 4,000 m

The midnight zone

No sunlight reaches here at all. The only light comes from animals. The water is about 4 °C, as cold as a fridge.

Light
Only from animals
Pressure at 4,000 m
About 400 times more than at the top

4,000 mto 6,000 m

The abyss

A wide, flat plain of soft mud, dark and close to freezing. Plains like this cover about half of the Earth's surface, and most of them have never been seen.

Light
Only from animals
Pressure at 6,000 m
About 600 times more than at the top

6,000 mto 11,000 m

The trenches

Long, deep cracks in the sea floor. The deepest place of all, the Challenger Deep, is almost 11 km down. Mount Everest would fit inside with room to spare.

Light
Only from animals
Pressure at 11,000 m
Over 1,000 times more than at the top
