Go insect spotting

The best insect spotters are slow, quiet and gentle. Here is where to look, and how to look without hurting anything.

Where to look

Under a log

Ants, earwigs and beetles hide where it is dark and damp.

On flowers

Bees, hoverflies and butterflies come to drink nectar.

By a pond

Pond skaters stand on the water. Dragonflies zoom above it.

In long grass

Listen for singing grasshoppers. Look for ladybirds on the stems.

Look, don't harm

  • Move slowly. Fast hands and a sudden shadow scare insects away.
  • Look, don't grab. For a closer look, let an insect walk onto a leaf.
  • Use a bug pot. A clear pot with air holes. Put the insect back where you found it after a few minutes.
  • Put logs back. Roll logs and stones back gently, the same way up, so the homes under them stay safe.
  • Wash your hands when you are done.

Never pick up stinging insects

Bees, wasps and hornets can sting. Watch them from a step away, and never put one in a bug pot.

If one flies close to you, stay calm and walk slowly away. Do not wave your arms.
