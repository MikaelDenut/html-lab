What makes an insect?

Every insect has three body parts and six legs. Look at the drawing, then hide the labels and test yourself.

Head
The front part. It holds the eyes, the antennae and the mouth.
Antennae
Two feelers on the head. Insects use them to smell and to touch.
Eyes
Big eyes made of many tiny parts. A dragonfly's eye has thousands of them.
Thorax
The middle part. All six legs and all the wings join on here.
Six legs
Three pairs, all on the thorax. Every grown-up insect has six.
Wings
Most grown-up insects have four. Flies have two, and worker ants have none.
Abdomen
The back part, with the stomach inside. A bee's or a wasp's sting is at the very end.
Exoskeleton
An insect has no bones. Its hard skin is its skeleton, on the outside!

Insect or not?

Count the legs and the body parts, then open each one to check.

A spider?

A spider has eight legs and only two body parts, so it is not an insect.

A woodlouse?

A woodlouse has 14 legs, so it is not an insect. It is a cousin of crabs and shrimps.

A centipede?

A centipede has far more than six legs, so it is not an insect. Funny thing: it never has exactly 100.

A ladybird?

Yes, a ladybird is an insect, with six legs, three body parts and four wings. The front two wings are its hard red cases.
