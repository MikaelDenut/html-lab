A

Ant

Ants live together in a nest, sometimes thousands of them. The workers are all sisters, and usually one queen lays all the eggs.

Where and when: under stones and paving, spring to autumn

B

Bumblebee

A big, furry bee. It shivers its flying muscles to warm up, so it can fly on cool days.

Where and when: flowers, March to October

Can sting: look, never touch

Butterfly

It tastes with its feet! It drinks nectar through a long tongue that curls up like a spring when it is not in use.

Where and when: sunny flowers, April to September

D

Dragonfly

It catches other insects in the air, and it can even fly backwards. A young dragonfly lives underwater, often for a year or two, before it gets its wings.

Where and when: ponds and rivers, May to September

E

Earwig

The pincers at its back end can only give a weak pinch. Earwig mothers guard their eggs all winter and keep them clean. And earwigs almost never go near ears. That story is a myth!

Where and when: under bark and flower pots, spring to autumn

G

Grasshopper

It sings by rubbing its back legs against its wings. Its ears are on the sides of its tummy.

Where and when: long grass, June to October

H

Hoverfly

Yellow and black stripes make it look like a wasp, but it cannot sting. It hangs still in the air, then zips away. Count the wings: a fly has only two.

Where and when: flowers, spring to autumn

L

Ladybird

A ladybird is a small beetle. Its bright spots warn birds that it tastes horrible. It eats greenfly, tiny bugs that suck the juice out of plants.

Where and when: leaves and nettles, April to October

M

Moth

Many moths fly at night and rest in the day. Moths often have feathery or thin antennae. A butterfly has a little club on the end of each one.

Where and when: near lights at night, all summer

P

Pond skater

It stands on top of the water! Tiny waterproof hairs on its legs stop it from sinking. It feels the ripples when an insect falls in, and races over to eat it.

Where and when: ponds and slow streams, spring to autumn

W

Wasp

A wasp chews wood into paper to build its nest. Wasps help gardens: they catch caterpillars and other bugs to feed their young.

Where and when: gardens and picnics, summer

Can sting: look, never touch
