Our garden year

We garden far in the north, at 60 degrees north. Winter is long and dark, summer is short and very bright. Here is what we sow, plant and pick each month.

The whole bar is one day and night. The yellow part is the daylight.

Spring

March

12 hours of daylight

Sow tomatoes indoors, on a sunny windowsill.

Do: wash last year's pots.

April

14 hours of daylight

Sow lettuce and cabbage indoors.

Do: put seed potatoes in a cool, bright room to grow short sprouts.

May

17 hours of daylight

Sow peas, carrots and radishes outside when the soil is dry.

Plant the sprouted potatoes.

Summer

June

19 hours of daylight

Plant tomatoes, courgettes and lettuce outside when the frosts are over.

Pick the first radishes.

July

18 hours of daylight

Pick peas, strawberries and lettuce.

Do: water early in the morning or in the evening.

August

16 hours of daylight

Pick carrots, potatoes, courgettes and tomatoes.

Do: let a few pea pods dry on the plant. They are next year's seeds.

Autumn

September

13 hours of daylight

Pick the last potatoes and the apples.

Plant garlic cloves, pointy end up. They need the winter cold.

October

10 hours of daylight

Plant tulip bulbs for spring flowers.

Do: rake leaves onto the empty beds, like a warm blanket.

November

7 hours of daylight

Do: clean, dry and oil the tools.

Do: empty the hoses before the water freezes.

Winter

December

6 hours of daylight

Do: make bird feeders and hang them by the garden.

January

7 hours of daylight

Do: draw a map of next year's beds and choose the seeds.

February

9 hours of daylight

Sow chillies indoors. They need a long time to grow.

Why do we sow some seeds indoors?

Tomatoes and chillies need a long, warm summer, and ours is short. So we start them on a warm windowsill in early spring. By June the plants are big and the frosts are over, and they can move outside.
